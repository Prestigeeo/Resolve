# Cybercrimes Act & Nigerian Banking Emergency Fraud Triage Protocols

## Statutory Authority
- **Enacting Bodies:** Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC), Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System (NIBSS).
- **Legal Foundation:** Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015; CBN Circular on Mandatory Fraud Desks and Interbank Post-No-Debit (PND) Liens.
- **Law Enforcement e-Reporting Portal:** https://incb.npf.gov.ng | Emergency Hotline: 112 / +234 809 993 7322

---

## Section 1: The Golden Hour — Emergency Fraud Interventions
When unauthorized transactions, phone theft, SIM swap, or social-engineering account takeover occur, funds move through multi-tier mule accounts within minutes. Consumers must take immediate sequential steps:
1. **Immediate Channel Kill-Switch (USSD Code):**
   - Dial the issuing bank's universal emergency USSD freeze code from ANY phone (not necessarily the registered SIM).
   - Major Bank Emergency Freeze Codes:
     - Access Bank: `*901*911#`
     - GTBank: `*737*51*10#`
     - Zenith Bank: `*966*911#`
     - FirstBank: `*894*911#`
     - United Bank for Africa (UBA): `*919*9#`
     - OPay: `*955*1310#`
     - PalmPay: `*861*0#`
     - Moniepoint: `*5573*911#`
     - Stanbic IBTC: `*909*911#`
     - Fidelity Bank: `*770*911#`
2. **Telco SIM Barring:**
   - Call the telecom provider to place an immediate bar on the stolen SIM card to prevent fraudsters from intercepting OTP codes or dialling USSD banking menus.
3. **Contacting the Originating Bank 24/7 Fraud Desk:**
   - Under CBN regulations, every commercial bank and payment switch in Nigeria must maintain an active 24/7 fraud desk connected to the NIBSS Central Anti-Fraud Portal.
   - The victim must report the unauthorized transfer immediately, requesting an emergency **Precautionary Post-No-Debit (PND)** hold on the recipient bank account.

## Section 2: Law Enforcement Filing & Fund Repatriation (NPF-NCCC)
1. **The Precautionary Hold vs. Permanent Repatriation:**
   - Banks can place an initial temporary administrative hold on suspected fraudulent recipient accounts for 24–72 hours via the NIBSS anti-fraud portal.
   - To make the hold permanent and order the receiving bank to repatriate the stolen funds back to the victim, banks and courts require a formal **Police Extract / e-Petition Reference** under Section 38 of the Cybercrimes Act 2015.
2. **Filing with the NPF-NCCC:**
   - Victims can lodge a report electronically through the NPF-NCCC portal at `https://incb.npf.gov.ng` or visit the nearest State Police Command / EFCC Zonal Office.
   - Documented requirements:
     - Bank Statement highlighting unauthorized debits.
     - Destination Account Number, Recipient Name, and Beneficiary Bank.
     - 30-digit NIBSS Session IDs for all fraudulent transfers.
     - Affidavit of Fact deposed at any Nigerian Court of Record.
